<CharDisplay character="一" label="A" reading="yī" />

The number one.

{/* 一也者，萬物之本也。 */}
{/* One is the foundation of all things. */}
{/* Attribution: From chapter 14 of the Huainanzi, “Sayings Explained.” The link opens that chapter on the Chinese Text Project. */}
<Quote id="L001A-Q01" />

It figures the primordial unity, source of all beings.

{/* 惟初太始，道立於一，造分天地，化成萬物。 */}
{/* At the very beginning, at the great origin, the Dao was established in One. It created and separated Heaven and Earth, transforming into all things. */}
{/* Attribution: From the entry for “one” in the received Shuowen Jiezi. The Chinese Text Project link highlights the passage in that entry. */}
<Quote id="L001A-Q02" />

It represents the unity, principle of numeration.

{/* 一者，數之始也，物之極也。 */}
{/* One is the origin of number and the utmost of things. */}
{/* Attribution: The words are from Wang Bi’s commentary on the Dao De Jing. The Chinese Text Project link highlights the commentary itself. */}
<Quote id="L001A-Q03" />

In composition, it is often figurative.

Its different figurative usages may be summed up under four principal categories:

<SectionBreak ordinal="First">
  when written on top of the compound, 一 represents either heaven, or a roof, or any cover.
</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="雨" label="B" reading="yǔ" />

The rain.

Drops of water falling from a 冂 cloud that hangs to 一 heaven; 丿 means the vertical falling.

<CharacterForms character="雨" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-03-oracle-bone-雨 sinica-08-bronze-雨 sinica-12-small-seal-雨" />

{/* 水从雲下也。一象天，冂象雲，水霝其閒也。 */}
{/* Water descends from the clouds. 一 represents heaven; 冂 represents a cloud; water falls between them. */}
{/* Attribution: From the Shuowen Jiezi entry for “rain.” The Chinese Text Project link highlights the definition and analysis in that entry. */}
<Quote id="L001B-Q01" />

It is the 173rd radical in the Kangxi.

<CharDisplay character="天" label="C" reading="tiān" />

Heaven.

The vast 一 extent of space that is above 大 men, the highest of things.

<CharacterForms character="天" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-02-oracle-bone-天 sinica-07-bronze-天 sinica-10-chu-slip-天" />

{/* 天顚也。至高無上,从一大。 */}
{/* 天 is the highest. It is supremely high, there is nothing above it. It is composed of 一 and 大. */}
{/* Attribution: From the Shuowen Jiezi entry for “Heaven.” The Chinese Text Project link highlights the received dictionary text. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q01" />

In this compound, the character 大 represents man,\
and not “great” as it later came to mean.

As explained by Zhu Junsheng:

{/* 按大猶人也，天在人上，仰首見之。 */}
{/* Note: 大 resembles a person. Heaven is above man; raising his head, he sees it. */}
{/* Attribution: From Zhu Junsheng’s Shuowen Tongxun Dingsheng, under “Heaven.” The link opens the scanned page on the Internet Archive. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q02" />

Placed above them, Heaven governs men.

{/* 居高理下，為人經緯，故其字一大以鎮之也。 */}
{/* It sits above and governs what is below, as warp and weft to man. Therefore, its written form: the sky [above] man, thus holding him in his place. */}
{/* Attribution: Attributed to the Chunqiu Shuoti Ci in the Jingdian Shiwen, vol. 29. The Chinese Text Project link opens the Jingdian Shiwen passage that preserves the quotation. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q03" />

Whence it is derived the idea of authority.

{/* 天子受命於天，諸侯受命於天子，子受命於父，臣妾受命於君，妻受命於夫。諸所受命者，其尊皆天也，雖謂受命於天亦可。 */}
{/* The Son of Heaven receives his mandate from Heaven; the feudal lords receive their mandates from the Son of Heaven; a son receives commands from his father; subjects and concubines receive commands from their lord; a wife receives commands from her husband. As for all those from whom decrees are received, their superior is, in all cases, Heaven. Even stating they receive their allotment from Heaven is also acceptable. */}
{/* Attribution: From “Following the Mandate” in Luxuriant Gems of the Spring and Autumn, a collection traditionally credited to Dong Zhongshu. The link opens the Chinese text of the chapter. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q04" />

Hence, any superior is the 天 of his inferior.

{/* 然則天亦可為凡顚之偁。臣於君、子於父、妻於夫、民於食皆曰天是也。 */}
{/* That being so, then 天 may likewise serve as the designation of whatsoever is at the summit. For instance: for a minister, his ruler; for a son, his father; for a wife, her husband; for the people, their food: all are called Heaven. */}
{/* Attribution: From Duan Yucai’s annotations to the Shuowen Jiezi entry for “Heaven.” The link highlights Duan’s note on Shuowen.org. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q05" />

---

<Commentary>
  Apart from a cover, the horizontal stroke 一 can also be interpreted as a highlighting device.
</Commentary>

<CharDisplay character="末" label="D" reading="mò" />

The top of a tree.

A horizontal stroke cuts through the top of a 木 tree,\
thus highlighting it.

<CharacterForms character="末" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-01-bronze-末 sinica-04-small-seal-末 sinica-08-clerical-末" />

{/* 木上曰末。从木，一在其上。 */}
{/* The top of a tree is called 末. From 木; 一 is above it. */}
{/* Attribution: From the Shuowen Jiezi entry for “tip.” The link opens the Wood radical section on the Chinese Text Project and highlights that entry. */}

Whence come its derived meanings.

</SectionBreak>

To stand, to be erected.\
A man 大 standing upon the 一 ground.

<CharacterForms character="立" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-03-bronze-立 sinica-09-chu-slip-立 sinica-12-small-seal-立 sinica-18-clerical-立" />

<CharDisplay character="本" label="G" reading="běn" />

The trunk of a tree.

A horizontal stroke cuts through the trunk of a 木 tree,\
thus highlighting it.

<CharacterForms character="本" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-01-bronze-本 sinica-06-small-seal-本 sinica-07-clerical-本 sinica-08-clerical-本" />

Alternatively, it means roots.\
The part of a 木 tree below the 一 earth.

{/* 木下曰本。从木，一在其下。 */}
{/* The bottom of a tree is called 本. From 木, a stroke is beneath it. */}
{/* Attribution: From the Shuowen Jiezi entry for “root.” The link opens the Wood radical section on the Chinese Text Project and highlights that entry. */}
<Quote id="L001G-Q01" />

The meanings of “trunk” and “roots”\
are whence come this character’s alternative meanings.

Morphologically, this character is the reverse of 末.

</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="丂" label="I" reading="kǎo" />

Difficulty in breathing.

The bent line represents the breath that tries to go out, but is checked by the transversal barrier.

<CharacterForms character="丂" description="Form comparison" historical="sinica-02-oracle-bone-丂 sinica-04-bronze-丂 sinica-05-small-seal-丂" />

{/* 气欲舒出。𠃑上礙於一也。 */}
{/* Air wishes to come out. */}
{/* The upward flow is blocked by the horizontal stroke. */}
{/* Attribution: From the Shuowen Jiezi entry discussed under “obstructed breath.” The Chinese Text Project link highlights the definition and analysis in the corresponding radical section. */}
<Quote id="L001I-Q01" />

<Commentary>
  It should be noted that this etymology and definition exists only in historical dictionaries, and **no literary use of this character exists** to either prove or disprove this genealogy.

  However, a few clues seem to exist in the historical forms of other characters.

  Take the following character, for example:

  <CharacterForms character="于" description="Seal and current forms of the character" historical="wikimedia-small-seal-于.svg" />

  Two etymologies exist for its historical form.

  **First,** an exhaled 丂 flat stream 一 of air.

  **Second,** an ancient wind instrument.

  For both of these etymologies, the core idea is that of a channeled stream of air.

  However, if the first etymology is to be considered, we should see the horizontal stroke not necessarily as a **hindrance**, but rather as the **origin** of the stream of air: such as a mouth that lets out its breath.

  Hence, and alternatively:
</Commentary>

Breath.

<CharacterFocus character="丂" />

The bent line represents the flow of breath, coming out from a mouth, represented by the horizontal stroke.

<SectionBreak ordinal="Fourth">
  一 represents something contained.
</SectionBreak>
