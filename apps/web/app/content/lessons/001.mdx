<CharDisplay character="一" label="A" />

<Quote sourcePending>
  <p lang="zh-Hant">爲記數之始</p>
  <p>Yi represents the unity, principle of numeration.</p>
</Quote>

It figures the primordial unity, source of all beings.

<Quote sourcePending>
  <p lang="zh-Hant">惟初太始道立於一造分天地化成萬物</p>
  <p>At the very beginning, at the great origin,<br/>the Dao was established in One.<br/>It created and separated Heaven and Earth,<br/>transforming into all things.</p>
</Quote>

In composition, says the *Shuowen*, it is mostly figurative.

<Quote sourcePending>
  <p lang="zh-Hant">一也者萬物之本也</p>
  <p>One is the foundation of all things.</p>
</Quote>

Its different figurative meanings may be summed up under four principal categories:

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-solid" />

<SectionBreak ordinal="First">
  When written on top of the compound, 一 represents either heaven, or a roof, or any cover.
</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="雨" label="B" />

The rain. Drops of water falling from a 冂 cloud that hangs to 一 heaven; 丿 means the vertical falling.

<CharacterForms character="雨" description="Old and new form of the character" />

<Quote source="Kangxi Zidian">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">一像天。冂像雲。水从雲下也。</p>
  <p>一 represents the sky. 冂 represents a cloud.<br/>Water flows down from the sky.</p>
</Quote>

It is the 173rd radical in the Kangxi.

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-dashed" />

<CharDisplay character="天" label="C" />

Heaven, the vast 一 extent of space that is above 大 men, the highest of things.

<CharacterForms character="天" description="Old and new form of the character" />

<Quote source="Shuowen Jiezi">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">天顚也。至高無上，从一大。</p>
  <p>天 is the highest.<br/>It is supremely high, there is nothing above it.<br/>It is composed of 一 and 大.</p>
</Quote>

In this compound, the character 大 represents man, and not “great” as it later came to mean.

As explained by Duan Yucai:

<Quote source="Shuowen Jiezi Zhu">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">按大猶人也。<br/>天在人上，仰首見之。<br/>一指事。</p>
  <p>Note: 大 means a person.<br/>Heaven is above man; when he raises his head, he sees it.<br/>一 is symbolic.</p>
</Quote>

Placed above them, Heaven governs men.

<Quote source="Bai Hu Tong">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">天之言鎮也。居高理下爲人經緯。<br/>故其字一大以鎮之也。</p>
  <p>Heaven’s word is to hold things in place.<br/>It sits above and governs what is below, as warp and weft to man.<br/>Thus, its character is 一 (the sky) above 大 (man), ruling over it.</p>
</Quote>

Whence it is derived the idea of authority.

<Quote source="Chunqiu Fanlu">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">天子受命于天，诸侯受命于天子，子受命于父，<br/>臣妾受命于君，妻受命于夫。<br/>诸所受命者，其尊皆天也，雖谓受命于天亦可。</p>
  <p>The Son of Heaven receives his mandate from Heaven.<br/>The feudal lords receive their mandates from the Son of Heaven.<br/>The son receives his mandate from the father.<br/>Subjects and concubines receive their mandates from their lord.<br/>The wife receives her mandate from the husband.<br/>All those who receive mandates—their superiors are all Heaven.<br/>Even saying their mandate was received from Heaven is also acceptable.</p>
</Quote>

Hence, any superior is the 天 of his inferior.

<Quote source="Chinese Characters (Wieger)">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">天君也。凡至尊重者皆是。<br/>故臣於君，子於父，妻於夫，皆曰天。</p>
  <p>Heaven is the ruler.<br/>All those who are supremely honored and important are so.<br/>Therefore: the ruler, by the minister; the father, by the son; the husband, by the wife —<br/>are all called heaven.</p>
</Quote>

<CharDisplay character="末" label="D" />

The top of a tree.

A horizontal stroke cuts through the top of a 木 tree, thus highlighting it.

<CharacterForms character="末" description="Old and new forms" />

<Quote source="Shuowen Jiezi">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">木上曰末。从木，一在其上。</p>
  <p>The top of a tree is called 末.<br/>From 木; one stroke is above it.</p>
</Quote>

Whence come its derived meanings.

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-solid" />

<SectionBreak ordinal="Second">
  When placed below the compound, 一 represents the foundation, the base, or any support.
</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="旦" label="E" />

The dawn, the beginning of the day. The 日 sun above the 一 horizon.

<CharacterForms character="旦" description="Old and new forms" />

<Quote source="Shuowen Jiezi">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">明也。从日見一上。一地也。</p>
  <p>It is light. From 日, as seen above a line. The stroke is the ground.</p>
</Quote>

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-dashed" />

<CharDisplay character="立" label="F" />

To stand, to be erected. A man 大 standing upon the 一 ground.

<CharacterForms character="立" description="Three historical forms" forms={3} />

<Quote source="Shuowen Jiezi">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">住也。从大立一之上。一地也。</p>
  <p>To stand still.<br/>Composed of a person standing above a horizontal stroke.</p>
</Quote>

Morphologically, this character is the reverse of 天.

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-dashed" />

<CharDisplay character="本" label="G" />

The trunk of a tree.

A horizontal stroke cuts through the trunk of a 木 tree, thus highlighting it.

<CharacterForms character="本" description="Old and new scripts" />

Alternatively, it means roots. The part of a 木 tree below the 一 earth.

<Quote source="Shuowen Jiezi">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">木下曰本。从木，一在其下。</p>
  <p></p>
</Quote>

The meanings of “trunk” and “roots” are whence come this character’s alternative meanings.

Morphologically, this character is the reverse of 末.

<SectionBreak ordinal="Third">
  一 often represents an obstruction or hindrance.
</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="閂" label="H" />

A beam 一 used to bolt a 門 door shut.

<CharacterForms character="閂" description="Form comparison" />

<Quote source="Kangxi Zidian">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">門橫關也。</p>
  <p>A door’s horizontal closure.</p>
</Quote>

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-dashed" />

<CharDisplay character="丂" label="I" />

Difficulty in breathing.

The bent line represents the breath that tries to go out, but is checked by the transversal barrier.

<CharacterForms character="丂" description="Form comparison" />

<Quote source="Shuowen Jiezi">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">气欲舒出。𠃑上礙於一也。</p>
  <p>Air wishes to come out.<br/>The upward flow is blocked by the horizontal stroke.</p>
</Quote>
