<CharDisplay character="一" label="A" />

{/* 一也者，萬物之本也。 */}
{/* One is the foundation of all things. */}
<Quote id="L001A-Q01" />

It figures the primordial unity, source of all beings.

{/* 惟初太始，道立於一，造分天地，化成萬物。 */}
{/* At the very beginning, at the great origin, the Dao was established in One. It created and separated Heaven and Earth, transforming into all things. */}
<Quote id="L001A-Q02" />

It represents the unity, principle of numeration.

{/* 一者，數之始也，物之極也。 */}
{/* One is the origin of number and the utmost of things. */}
<Quote id="L001A-Q03" />

In composition, it is often figurative.

Its different figurative usages may be summed up under four principal categories:

<SectionBreak ordinal="First">
  When written on top of the compound, 一 represents either heaven, or a roof, or any cover.
</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="雨" label="B" />

The rain. Drops of water falling from a 冂 cloud that hangs to 一 heaven; 丿 means the vertical falling.

<CharacterForms character="雨" description="Old and new form of the character" />

{/* 水从雲下也。一象天，冂象雲，水霝其閒也。 */}
{/* Water descends from the clouds. 一 represents heaven; 冂 represents a cloud; water falls between them. */}
<Quote id="L001B-Q01" />

It is the 173rd radical in the Kangxi.

<CharDisplay character="天" label="C" />

Heaven, the vast 一 extent of space that is above 大 men, the highest of things.

<CharacterForms character="天" description="Old and new form of the character" />

{/* 天顚也。至高無上,从一大。 */}
{/* 天 is the highest. It is supremely high, there is nothing above it. It is composed of 一 and 大. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q01" />

In this compound, the character 大 represents man, and not “great” as it later came to mean.

As explained by Zhu Junsheng:

{/* 按大猶人也，天在人上，仰首見之。 */}
{/* Note: 大 resembles a person. Heaven is above man; raising his head, he sees it. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q02" />

Placed above them, Heaven governs men.

{/* 居高理下，為人經緯，故其字一大以鎮之也。 */}
{/* It sits above and governs what is below, as warp and weft to man. Therefore, its written form: the sky [above] man, thus holding him in his place. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q03" />

Whence it is derived the idea of authority.

{/* 天子受命於天，諸侯受命於天子，子受命於父，臣妾受命於君，妻受命於夫。諸所受命者，其尊皆天也，雖謂受命於天亦可。 */}
{/* The Son of Heaven receives his mandate from Heaven; the feudal lords receive their mandates from the Son of Heaven; a son receives commands from his father; subjects and concubines receive commands from their lord; a wife receives commands from her husband. As for all those from whom decrees are received, their superior is, in all cases, Heaven. Even stating they receive their allotment from Heaven is also acceptable. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q04" />

Hence, any superior is the 天 of his inferior.

{/* 然則天亦可為凡顚之偁。臣於君、子於父、妻於夫、民於食皆曰天是也。 */}
{/* That being so, then 天 may likewise serve as the designation of whatsoever is at the summit. For instance: for a minister, his ruler; for a son, his father; for a wife, her husband; for the people, their food: all are called Heaven. */}
<Quote id="L001C-Q05" />

---

<CharDisplay character="末" label="D" />

<CharacterForms character="末" description="Old and new forms" />

<CharDisplay character="丂" label="I" />

Difficulty in breathing.

The bent line represents the breath that tries to go out, but is checked by the transversal barrier.

<CharacterForms character="丂" description="Form comparison" />
