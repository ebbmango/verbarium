<CharDisplay character="一" label="A" />

<LegacyQuote source="The Wenzi" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/wenzi/dao-de#n58586">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">一也者，萬物之本也。</p>
  <p>One is the root of all things.</p>
</LegacyQuote>

It figures the primordial unity, source of all beings.

{/* 惟初太始，道立於一，造分天地，化成萬物。 */}
{/* At the very beginning, at the great origin, the Dao was established in One. It created and separated Heaven and Earth, transforming into all things. */}
<Quote quote={shuowenJieziOnOne} sourceHref="https://ctext.org/shuo-wen-jie-zi/yi-bu#n26162" />

It represents the unity, principle of numeration.

{/* 一者，數之始也，物之極也。 */}
{/* One is the origin of number and the utmost of things. */}
<Quote quote={wangBiNotesOnOne} sourceHref="https://ctext.org/dao-de-zhen-jing-zhu#n90518" />

In composition, it is often figurative.

Its different figurative usages may be summed up under four principal categories:

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-solid" />

<SectionBreak ordinal="First">
  When written on top of the compound, 一 represents either heaven, or a roof, or any cover.
</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="雨" label="B" />

The rain. Drops of water falling from a 冂 cloud that hangs to 一 heaven; 丿 means the vertical falling.

<CharacterForms character="雨" description="Old and new form of the character" />

<LegacyQuote source="Kangxi Zidian">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">一像天。冂像雲。水从雲下也。</p>
  <p>一 represents the sky. 冂 represents a cloud.<br/>Water flows down from the sky.</p>
</LegacyQuote>

It is the 173rd radical in the Kangxi.

<hr className="lesson-divider lesson-divider-dashed" />

<CharDisplay character="天" label="C" />

Heaven, the vast 一 extent of space that is above 大 men, the highest of things.

<CharacterForms character="天" description="Old and new form of the character" />

<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/shuo-wen-jie-zi/yi-bu/zh#:~:text=%E5%A4%A9%EF%BC%9A%E9%A1%9A%E4%B9%9F%E3%80%82%E8%87%B3%E9%AB%98%E7%84%A1%E4%B8%8A%EF%BC%8C%E4%BB%8E%E4%B8%80%E3%80%81%E5%A4%A7%E3%80%82">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">天顚也。至高無上，从一大。</p>
  <p>天 is the highest.<br/>It is supremely high, there is nothing above it.<br/>It is composed of 一 and 大.</p>
</LegacyQuote>

In this compound, the character 大 represents man, and not “great” as it later came to mean.

As explained in the Shuowen Tongxun Dingsheng:

<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Tongxun Dingsheng" sourceHref="https://lib.ntua.edu.tw/wSite/public/Data/f1717122773363.pdf">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">按大猶人也，天在人上，仰首見之。</p>
  <p>Note: 大 means a person.<br/>Heaven is above man; when he raises his head, he sees it.</p>
</LegacyQuote>

Placed above them, Heaven governs men.

<LegacyQuote source="Jingdian Shiwen" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=604424&if=gb#:~:text=%E5%B1%85%E9%AB%98%E7%90%86%E4%B8%8B%EF%BC%8C%E7%82%BA%E4%BA%BA%E7%B6%93%E7%B7%AF%EF%BC%8C%E6%95%85%E5%85%B6%E5%AD%97%E4%B8%80%E5%A4%A7%E4%BB%A5%E9%8E%AE%E4%B9%8B%E4%B9%9F%E3%80%82">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">居高理下，為人經緯，故其字一大以鎮之也。</p>
  <p>It sits above and governs what is below, as warp and weft to people.<br/>Thus, its character is 一 (the sky) above 大 (man), ruling over it.</p>
</LegacyQuote>

Whence it is derived the idea of authority.

<LegacyQuote source="Chunqiu Fanlu" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/chun-qiu-fan-lu/shun-ming/zh#:~:text=%E5%A4%A9%E5%AD%90%E5%8F%97%E5%91%BD%E6%96%BC%E5%A4%A9%EF%BC%8C%E8%AB%B8%E4%BE%AF%E5%8F%97%E5%91%BD%E6%96%BC%E5%A4%A9%E5%AD%90%EF%BC%8C%E5%AD%90%E5%8F%97%E5%91%BD%E6%96%BC%E7%88%B6%EF%BC%8C%E8%87%A3%E5%A6%BE%E5%8F%97%E5%91%BD%E6%96%BC%E5%90%9B%EF%BC%8C%E5%A6%BB%E5%8F%97%E5%91%BD%E6%96%BC%E5%A4%AB%E3%80%82%E8%AB%B8%E6%89%80%E5%8F%97%E5%91%BD%E8%80%85%EF%BC%8C%E5%85%B6%E5%B0%8A%E7%9A%86%E5%A4%A9%E4%B9%9F%EF%BC%8C%E9%9B%96%E8%AC%82%E5%8F%97%E5%91%BD%E6%96%BC%E5%A4%A9%E4%BA%A6%E5%8F%AF%E3%80%82">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">天子受命於天，諸侯受命於天子，子受命於父，<br/>臣妾受命於君，妻受命於夫。<br/>諸所受命者，其尊皆天也，雖謂受命於天亦可。</p>
  <p>The Son of Heaven receives his mandate from Heaven; the feudal lords receive their mandates from the Son of Heaven; a son receives his mandate from his father;<br/>ministers and concubines receive their mandates from their lord; a wife receives her mandate from her husband.<br/>Those from whom such mandates are received, as the honored superiors, are all “Heaven”; thus one may also say that the mandate is received from Heaven.</p>
</LegacyQuote>

Hence, any superior is the 天 of his inferior.

<LegacyQuote source="Duan Yucai, Shuowen Jiezi Zhu" sourceHref="https://www.shuowen.org/view/3">
  <p lang="zh-Hant">然則天亦可為凡顚之偁。<br/>臣於君、子於父、妻於夫、民於食皆曰天是也。</p>
  <p>Thus 天 may also serve as a general designation for whatever stands at the top:<br/>for a minister, the ruler; for a son, the father; for a wife, the husband; and for the people, food.<br/>All of these are called 天 (‘Heaven’).</p>
</LegacyQuote>

</LegacyQuote>

Whence come its derived meanings.

<CharDisplay character="立" label="F" />

To stand, to be erected. A man 大 standing upon the 一 ground.

<CharacterForms character="立" description="Three historical forms" forms={3} />

<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/shuo-wen-jie-zi/li-bu7/zh#:~:text=%E7%AB%8B%EF%BC%9A%E4%BD%8F%E4%B9%9F%E3%80%82%E4%BB%8E%E5%A4%A7%E7%AB%8B%E4%B8%80%E4%B9%8B%E4%B8%8A%E3%80%82">

Difficulty in breathing.

<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi">

</LegacyQuote>
