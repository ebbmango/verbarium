<LessonHeader number={3}>
About 三 and its derivatives.
</LessonHeader>

<CharDisplay character="三" label="A" reading="sān" />

Three.

The number of humanity.

{/* L003A-Q01 · Wieger: 天地人之數也。 · found/verbatim (Wei Xiao, Liushu Jingyun, juan 1, entry 三; the Ming print has 天地人㞢數也, 㞢 written 之 here). The received Shuowen reads 天地人之道也 (道, not 數). Wei's entry goes on 自一而再重㞢，三才葡矣 (葡, 備 in the top margin): Wieger's “three Powers”. */}
{/* Attribution: From Wei Xiao’s entry for “three” in the Liushu Jingyun, vol. 1. The Chinese Text Project link opens scan page 49: under the large seal 三, column 4 from the right, around the middle of the page. */}
<LegacyQuote
  source="Liushu Jingyun"
  sourceDetails="Wei Xiao · vol. 1"
  sourceHref="https://ctext.org/library.pl?if=en&file=13795&page=49"

  天地人之數也。

  The number of Heaven, Earth, and humanity.
</LegacyQuote>


Because it makes a triad\
with the Heaven and the Earth.

---

Whence:

<CharDisplay character="王" label="B" reading="wáng" />

King.

<CharacterForms character="王" description="Oracle-bone, clerical, and current forms of 王" historical="sinica-02-oracle-bone-王 sinica-04-oracle-bone-王 sinica-05-oracle-bone-王 sinica-28-clerical-王" />

According to the ancients,\
the king is the *one* who connects together\
*Heaven,* *Earth* and *Humanity.*

{/* L003B-Q01 · Wieger: 古之造文者。三畫而連其中。謂之王。三者。天地人也。而參通之者。王也。 · found/verbatim (received Shuowen, entry 王, quoting Dong Zhongshu; Wieger drops 董仲舒曰 and the definition 天下所歸往也). The Chunqiu Fanlu, “Wangdao Tong San”, has related wording with 三書. */}
{/* Attribution: Dong Zhongshu’s words, as the received Shuowen Jiezi quotes them in its entry for “king.” The Chinese Text Project link highlights the passage in the King radical section. */}
<LegacyQuote
  source="Shuowen Jiezi"
  sourceDetails="Dong Zhongshu"
  sourceHref="https://ctext.org/shuo-wen-jie-zi/wang-bu#:~:text=%E5%8F%A4%E4%B9%8B%E9%80%A0%E6%96%87%E8%80%85"
>
  三者，天、地、人也，\
  而參通之者，王也。

  The three strokes are Heaven, Earth, and humanity;\
  the one who joins and passes through them is the king.
</LegacyQuote>

It may also represent\
boundaries, demarcations, or limits:

<CharDisplay character="畺" label="C" reading="jiāng" />

Bounds.

The 三 partitions that divide\
and limit two 田 fields.

  source="Shuowen Jiezi"

When straightened up vertically,\
it forms a part of:

<CharDisplay character="示" label="D" reading="shì" />

To reveal.

<CharacterForms character="示" description="Oracle-bone and small-seal forms of 示">
    sinica-05-oracle-bone-示 sinica-07-oracle-bone-示 sinica-06-oracle-bone-示 sinica-15-small-seal-示
</CharacterForms>

An influx coming from above,\
of auspicious or inauspicious signs,\
by which the will of Heaven is\
*revealed* to mankind.

<CharacterForms character="示" description="Small-seal, Chu-slip, clerical, and current forms of 示">
    sinica-16-small-seal-示 sinica-12-chu-slip-示 sinica-17-clerical-示 current
</CharacterForms>

The two horizontal lines\
are the old form of 上, *high.*

<CharacterFocus character="示" highlight="1 2" />

Here they mean *Heaven.*

{/* L003D-Q01 · Wieger: 天垂像。見吉凶。所以示人也。 · found/verbatim (received Shuowen, entry 示; also Zhu, entry 示, leaf n732). Every witness writes 象; Wieger's 像 is in none of them. Shown here with the source's 象. */}
{/* Attribution: From the received Shuowen Jiezi entry for “to show.” The link opens that entry on Shuowen.org. */}
<LegacyQuote
  source="Shuowen Jiezi"
  sourceHref="https://www.shuowen.org/view/10"
>
  天垂象，見吉凶，所以示人也。

  Heaven hangs down images,\
  showing good and bad fortune;\
  this is how it shows them to people.
</LegacyQuote>

The three vertical lines\
represent what hangs from heaven:

<CharacterFocus character="示" highlight="3 4 5"  />

The sun, the moon, and the stars;\
whose changes reveal to men\
the transcendent things.

{/* L003D-Q03 · Wieger: 三垂。日月星也。觀乎天文以察時變。示神事也。 · found/verbatim (received Shuowen, entry 示; also Zhu, entry 示, leaf n732). 觀乎天文，以察時變 is the Zhouyi, Tuan on hexagram 22 賁, carried by the Shuowen. */}

<LegacyQuote
  source="Shuowen Jiezi"
  sourceHref="https://www.shuowen.org/view/10"
>
  三垂，\
  日、月、星也。\
  觀乎天文，以察時變。

  The three hanging strokes\
  are the sun, the moon, and the stars.\
  One observes the patterns of Heaven\
  to examine the changes of the seasons.
</LegacyQuote>

It forms the 113th radical,\
of characters relating to transcendental matters.

When doubled, 示 forms:

<CharDisplay character="祘" label="E" reading="suàn" />

To calculate.

<CharacterForms character="祘" description="Small-seal and current forms of 祘 (to calculate). In the seal form, each half has two crossbars above three hanging strokes." historical="sinica-01-small-seal-祘" />

It is believed that this character figures\
the primitive abacus,\
and thus has nothing to do with 示.

<Commentary>
Although this is a common theory,\
the character is also believed to predate the\
invention of the abacus.

Thus, Duan Yucai's\
alternative interpretation may be useful:

{/* L003E-Q02 · Duan Yucai’s comment on Xu Shen’s definition, printed below the entry on the linked page. */}
<LegacyQuote
  source="Shuowen Jiezi Zhu"
  sourceDetails="Duan Yucai"
  sourceHref="https://www.shuowen.org/view/70#:~:text=明視故从二示"
>
  明視，故从二示。

  To see clearly, hence the use of two 示.
</LegacyQuote>

To discern its will, one must\
carefully observe the signs of Heaven.

To *count*, *calculate* or *plan*,\
one must carefully observe its object of study.
</Commentary>
