<CharDisplay character="二" label="A" reading="èr" />

Two.

The number of the earth, \
because it makes a pair with heaven.

{/* L002A-Q01 · Wieger: 地之數也。 · found/verbatim (received Shuowen, entry 二; also the head of Zhu's entry 二) */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/shuo-wen-jie-zi/er-bu3#:~:text=%E4%BA%8C%EF%BC%9A%E5%9C%B0%E4%B9%8B%E6%95%B8%E4%B9%9F">
  地之數也。从偶一。

  The number of Earth. \
  Formed from a pair of ones.
</LegacyQuote>

The number of the two principles.

{/* L002A-Q02 · Wieger: 陰陽之數也。 · found/verbatim (Zhu, entry 二, quoting Shuoyuan 辨物; Wieger drops 二者) */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuoyuan" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=22109&show=parallel#:~:text=%E4%BA%8C%E8%80%85%E9%99%B0%E9%99%BD%E4%B9%8B%E6%95%B8%E4%B9%9F">
  二者陰陽之數也。

  Two, the number of Yin and Yang.
</LegacyQuote>

In composition, it has mainly three different uses:

<SectionBreak ordinal="First">
  it means *two*. For example:
</SectionBreak>

<CharDisplay character="仁" label="B" reading="rén" />

Benevolence.

<CharacterForms character="仁" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-10-small-seal-仁" />

The fundamental virtue of Confucianism,\
ordinarily translated as *humaneness.*

{/* L002B-Q01 · Wieger: 親也。从人、从二、會意。相親謂之仁。 · found/paraphrase: opening = received Shuowen, entry 仁 (Zhu adds 會意) */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/shuo-wen-jie-zi/ren-bu1#:~:text=%E4%BB%81%EF%BC%9A%E8%A6%AA%E4%B9%9F%E3%80%82%E4%BB%8E%E4%BA%BA%E4%BB%8E%E4%BA%8C%E3%80%82">
  親也。从人从二。

  Kinship. Formed from 人 and from 二.
</LegacyQuote>

To love each other.

The benevolence that must link each\
亻 man with 二 his neighbour.

{/* L002B-Q02 · Wieger's tail 相親謂之仁 = Liji 經解, as Zhu quotes it in entry 仁 (Wieger drops 上下) */}
<LegacyQuote source="Liji" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=61476&show=parallel#:~:text=%E4%B8%8A%E4%B8%8B%E7%9B%B8%E8%A6%AA%EF%BC%8C%E8%AC%82%E4%B9%8B%E4%BB%81">
  上下相親，謂之仁。

  High and low hold one another dear,\
  this is called love.
</LegacyQuote>

<Commentary>
  The very principle by which Heaven and Earth\
  bring forth living things.

  {/* L002B-Q03 · Li Shigu, Liji Zhongyao, juan 25, Jingjie; excerpt from his 按 note, not the ancient Liji. */}
  <LegacyQuote source="Liji Zhongyao" sourceHref="https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/CADAL02033688/chapter/1lalnghmf0xex">
    純乎天地生物之理。

    Wholly in accord with the principle by which\
    Heaven and Earth bring forth life.
  </LegacyQuote>
</Commentary>

From 仁 it is derived:

<CharDisplay character="佞" label="C" reading="nìng" />

Coaxing.

<CharacterForms character="佞" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-01-small-seal-佞" />

{/* L002C-Q01 · Wieger: 巧諂也。 · found/paraphrase (3rd, 4th and 7th characters of Xu Kai's sentence) */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi Xizhuan" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/library.pl?if=en&file=77400&page=87">
  佞者巧諂高才也。

  The smooth-tongued is\
  artful in flattery, of great talent.
</LegacyQuote>

Flattery, the fellowship of women.

The authors of the Chinese characters have been hard on the daughters of Eve.

{/* L002C-Q02 · probable source of Wieger's English "the 仁 of 女 women"; a few lines after Q01 */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi Xizhuan" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/library.pl?if=en&file=77400&page=88">
  佞者女子之仁，\
  故于文女仁為佞。

  Fawning is a womanly kindliness;\
  thus, in writing, 女 and 仁 make 佞.
</LegacyQuote>

<Commentary>
  I think the etymology runs deeper here.

  Xu Kai himself says:

  {/* L002C-Q03 · Same passage as Q01, CText file 77400, p. 87, columns 7–8. */}
  <LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi Xizhuan" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/library.pl?if=en&file=77400&page=87">
    有小才不以正道近其君，\
    曰佞也。

    One who possesses some talent but uses improper means to gain closeness to his ruler is called 佞.
  </LegacyQuote>

  The aim is to win someone's goodwill through flattery.

  To my mind, this suggests *seduction:*\
  the way a 女 woman might seek to win a man's 仁 affection.
</Commentary>

<Commentary>
  {/* L002C-Q04 · Kong Yingda’s subcommentary on Duke Cheng, year 13, 寡人不佞 (“I am without talent”); not the Zuozhuan base text or Du Yu’s note. */}
  <LegacyQuote source="Chunqiu Zuozhuan Zhengyi" sourceHref="https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B%E5%B7%A6%E5%82%B3%E6%AD%A3%E7%BE%A9/%E5%8D%B727#成公十有三年傳">
    佞是口才捷利之名，\
    本非善惡之稱。

    佞 is the name for quick and sharp verbal talent;\
    in itself not a designation of good or evil.
  </LegacyQuote>

  It can also mean *eloquence*,\
  whether used for good or ill.
</Commentary>

<CharDisplay character="亟" label="D" reading="jí" />

Activity, working up of faculties.\
Struggle for life.

<CharacterForms character="亟" description="Old and new forms of the character" historical="sinica-11-small-seal-亟" />

A <InlineForm name="sinica-12-chu-slip-人" character="人" /> man who struggles,\
with his <InlineForm name="口-bronze.svg" character="口" /> mouth and his <InlineForm name="又-bigseal.svg" character="又" /> hand,\
between <InlineForm name="二-bigseal.svg" character="二" /> heaven and earth.

{/* L002D-Q01 · Wieger: 从人、从口、从[hand]、會意。从二、天地也、指事。人生天地間、手口並作敏疾成事也。 · found/verbatim (Zhu, entry 亟; Wieger prints 二 once) */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Tongxun Dingsheng" sourceHref="https://archive.org/details/shuowentongxundi110zhuj/page/n852/mode/1up">
  人生天地間，手口並作，敏疾成事也。

  Man lives between Heaven and Earth;\
  hand and mouth working together,\
  he swiftly brings his affairs to completion.
</LegacyQuote>

<Commentary>
  Following the Shuowen tradition,\
  Wieger is correct in his graphemic breakdown.

  However, no attested use of 亟 corroborates the meanings of *"activity"* or *"struggle for life".*

  Instead, its most well-attested meaning is:

  <CharacterFocus character="亟" />

  Urgency.

  {/* L002D-Q02 · Songshi, juan 412, biography of Wang Deng; Wenyuan-ge Siku transcription, leaf 22a (邉 normalized to 邊). */}
  <LegacyQuote source="Songshi" sourceHref="https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/宋史/卷412#王登">
    邊事甚亟。

    Affairs on the frontier were very urgent.
  </LegacyQuote>

  Wieger's hazy definition captures the outward appearance of urgent action.
</Commentary>

<Commentary>
  Its earliest forms depict a person,\
  its extremities marked by two strokes.

  <CharacterForms description="Oracle-bone form of 亟" forms={1} historical="sinica-02-oracle-bone-亟" />

  The mouth and hand were added later.

  <CharacterForms description="Bronze forms of 亟" forms={3} historical="sinica-03-bronze-亟 sinica-04-bronze-亟 sinica-05-bronze-亟" />

  <ChapterStart />

  Although it later came to mean *urgent*,\
  the meanings of *extreme* and *limit*\
  are still preserved in:

  <CharDisplay character="極" label="補" reading="jí" />

  Extreme.

  {/* L002D-Q03 · Liji, Quli I, section 2. */}
  <LegacyQuote source="Liji" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=9482&show=parallel">
    樂不可極。

    Pleasure must not be carried to extremes.
  </LegacyQuote>

  <CharacterForms character="極" description="Bronze, small-seal, and clerical forms of 極" historical="極-seal.svg 極-bronze-spring.svg sinica-92-E80A-clerical-極 sinica-92-E80B-clerical-極" />

  {/* L002D-Q04 · Wenzi, Shang Li (Upper Rites), section 3. */}
  <LegacyQuote source="Wenzi" sourceHref="https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=58695&show=parallel">
    天地之道，\
    極則反，益則損。

    The way of Heaven and Earth:\
    on reaching its limit, it turns back;\
    on increasing, it diminishes.
  </LegacyQuote>  
</Commentary>

<CharDisplay character="亙" label="E" reading="gèn" />

A boat traverses between two shores.

<CharacterForms character="亙" description="Bronze and small-seal forms of 亙" historical="sinica-03-bronze-亙 sinica-06-small-seal-亙" />

{/* L002E-Q01 · Wieger: 从二、从舟、會意. 兼指事。二者上下厓岸也。 · found/verbatim (Zhu, entry 亙, his own 按 note; Wieger moves 會意兼指事 forward) */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Tongxun Dingsheng" sourceHref="https://archive.org/details/shuowentongxundi110zhuj/page/n314/mode/1up">
  从二从舟。二者上下厓岸也。

  After two strokes, after a boat.\
  The two strokes are the upper and lower cliffsides, riverbanks.
</LegacyQuote>

<Commentary>
  *Extension;*\
  through either space:

  <LegacyQuote
    source="Zhang Heng"
    sourceDetails="Rhapsody on the Western Capital"
    sourceHref="https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/CADAL01026760/chapter/1l3eqak13kbvq"
  >
   亙雄虹之長梁，\
   結棼橑以相接。

  Long beams span like mighty rainbows,\
  the interlacing rafters join together.
  </LegacyQuote>  

  or time, hence:\
  *Duration.*

  <LegacyQuote
    source="Zhu Xi"
    sourceDetails="Collected Writings, vol. 36."
    sourceHref="https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SBCK218/chapter/SBCK218_1542"
  >
    亙古亙今只是一體。

  Extending through antiquity\
  and through the present,\
  it is a single whole.
  </LegacyQuote>  

  Also, by extension:\
  To *permeate,* or to *exhaust.*

  <LegacyQuote
    source="Zhang Heng"
    sourceDetails="Rhapsody on the Southern Capital"
    sourceHref="https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SBCK300/chapter/1j73qvv6nmtyy_18"
  >
  貯水渟洿，亙望無涯。

  Water collects in still pools;\
  as far as the eye can see, there is no shore.
  </LegacyQuote>

  To *surround.*

  <LegacyQuote
    source="Zuo Si"
    sourceDetails="Rhapsody on the Capital of Wu"
    sourceHref="https://zh.wikisource.org/zh/%E5%90%B3%E9%83%BD%E8%B3%A6"
  >
  樹以青槐，亙以綠水。

  Planted with green locust trees,\
  and encircled with green waters.
  </LegacyQuote>

</Commentary>

<CharDisplay character="恆" label="F" reading="héng" />

Constancy.

<CharacterForms character="恆" description="Bronze, small-seal, clerical, and current forms of 恆" historical="sinica-03-bronze-恆 sinica-12-small-seal-恆 sinica-13-clerical-恆 sinica-17-clerical-恆 sinica-19-clerical-恆" />

The heart which sees a crossing through.

{/* L002F-Q01 · Wieger: 常也。从心、从舟、在二之間。上下一、必以舟旋航也。 · partial (航 in no text read); below is the received Shuowen, entry 恆, the text behind it */}
<LegacyQuote source="Shuowen Jiezi" sourceHref="https://www.shuowen.org/view/8970">
  常也。\
  从心从舟，在二之閒上下。\

  Constancy.\
  After a heart, after a boat,\
  between two strokes: one above, one below.
</LegacyQuote>

  it is an older form of *up*, *high*:

an older form of *down*, *low*:
